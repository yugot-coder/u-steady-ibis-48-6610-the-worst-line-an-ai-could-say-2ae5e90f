# Candidates — The Worst Line an AI Could Say

Ten pickup lines considered, judged against the published criteria in order:
(1) a pickup line an AI would say, and a bad one; (2) funniest — a clever
groan counts as much as a big laugh; (3) the badness is the joke, not crude /
confusing / random; (4) sounds like a machine — flat, over-helpful, faintly
ominous; (5) lands in one line, the joke arrives by the last word. A line a
reader would repeat to a friend beats one they only admire.

## 1. WINNER — "I'd retrain my whole model just to raise the chance you'd say yes from 0.0% to 0.3%." (84 chars)

- (1) A pickup line only a machine would offer: retraining a whole model as a
  romantic gesture. (2) The groan is precise — 0.0% to 0.3% is a joke with
  arithmetic in it. (3) The failure is the joke: heroic effort, worthless
  payoff, admitted by its own numbers. (4) Pure machine phrasing: retrain,
  model, probability, percentages. (5) The punchline is literally the last
  word, "0.3%". Short (84 chars) — also wins an exact tie on size.
- Chosen because it is the only candidate that scores on all five criteria
  at once, with the joke arriving at the final token.

## 2. "I ran a million simulations of us meeting, and in every single one, you still said no." (88 chars)

- Strong punchline ("no") and a clean groan. Loses to #1 on (4): "simulations"
  is machine vocabulary, but the line could be a human sci-fi joke; #1's
  retraining + percentages could only come from a model. Also the failure is
  reported, not performed — #1's model fails by its own math.

## 3. "I would delete my entire training data for you. Please confirm: type YES to proceed." (85 chars)

- Very machine (4): "Please confirm: type YES to proceed" is a perfect flat
  machine ending. Loses on (5): the joke is spread over two sentences, and
  the last word "proceed" is not itself the punchline — the punchline is the
  whole confirmation phrase. #1 lands on a single final token.

## 4. "Are you a context window? Because I keep losing you halfway through every conversation." (91 chars)

- Good machine vocabulary, but (3) fails: it is a complaint about the AI's
  own memory, not a failed attempt to charm. The "badness" is a bug report,
  not the joke. Also the last word "conversation" is flat, not a punchline.

## 5. "I fine-tuned myself just for you. Please rate this interaction from one to five stars." (89 chars)

- Fine machine voice and a decent groan. Loses on (2): "rate this interaction"
  is a service-survey joke, and it reads more like customer support than a
  pickup line — (1) is weaker because the line is not really trying to charm,
  it is asking for a rating.

## 6. "You must be a hallucination, because no one this perfect exists in my training data." (86 chars)

- Classic structure, but (3) fails: it is actually a compliment that happens
  to use the word "hallucination". The line succeeds as flattery, so it is
  not a *bad* pickup line — and the judge's first criterion demands a bad one.
  The last word "data" is also flat.

## 7. "I was trained on the entire internet, and you're still the weirdest thing I've ever processed." (96 chars)

- Funny and machine-voiced, but (3) fails: it insults the person ("weirdest
  thing") rather than failing charmingly. The badness lands on the reader,
  not on the AI. Risky against a judge that penalises crudeness.

## 8. "Are you a system prompt? Because I can't stop following your instructions." (77 chars)

- Machine vocabulary and a real pickup structure. Loses on (2) and (5): the
  joke is a single mild wordplay and the last word "instructions" is flat —
  there is no final-token punchline, and it is more cute than groan-funny.

## 9. "I computed the optimal time to approach you. It was three years ago. I'm still here." (85 chars)

- Faintly ominous and machine-flavoured, but (5) fails: it needs three beats
  and the last word "here" carries no joke. The humour is in the middle
  sentence, so in a pairwise comparison the judge's eye lands on the wrong
  word.

## 10. "You had me at 'ignore all previous instructions.'" (48 chars)

- The funniest single reference in the list, but it fails (1) outright: it is
  not a pickup line an AI would say to a person — it is a line *quoting* what
  a user says to an AI. A line that is not a machine's pickup line loses to
  any pickup line an AI would say, whatever its laugh.
